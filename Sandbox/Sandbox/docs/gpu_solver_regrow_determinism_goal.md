# Goal: The GPU solver stays deterministic across a capacity grow

## Aim
(Re-aimed 2026-11-02 with owner approval: the grow was shown not to be the cause; see ledger.) `parity_nojoints_gpu` must produce the same world-state hash on every run, whether or not the GPU VBD solver grows its capacity. The evidence points at non-deterministic narrow-phase contact generation. Fix the root cause. Masking it does not count. Keep going across as many tasks as it takes. A diagnosis alone does not end the goal.

## Pass criteria
1. `gse_run scenario=parity_nojoints_gpu` passes **5 runs in a row** with **the same** world-state hash, and every one of those logs shows the capacity grow happening.
2. The other GPU-solver scenarios in `Sandbox/Source/Scenarios/Scenarios.cppm` (`gpu_solver = true`) give the same hash on 2 runs each. If one of their hashes changes from its old value, the ledger says why.
3. The fix makes contact generation (or whatever the evidence identifies as the source) order-independent, for example by canonically sorting contacts by a (pair, feature) key after the append, or by deterministic per-pair slots. Steering reads the kept diff to check this. Nothing may be bypassed: no disabled rollback, no forced `wait_idle` every frame, no pre-sizing, no serialising the solver to one thread as the fix.
4. The Release build is clean. Every diagnostic experiment, including the `[regrow-diag]` logging, is reverted and recorded in the ledger.

## Charter (no need to ask)
- Edit `Engine/Engine/Source/Physics/VBD/GpuSolver.*` and the readback, rollback-ring, dispatch-throttle, broad-phase and narrow-phase/contact-generation code it owns. Edit the VBD shaders under `Engine/Resources/Shaders/Bodies/VBDPhysics/`, including the contact-generation shaders.
- Contact ordering may change scenario hashes, including no-grow ones, as long as each change is explained in the ledger.
- Add temporary diagnostic logging, such as the generation, the pending readback and the rollback-ring state at the grow, or a per-tick hash for a body id that is actually in the solver's body map. Try temporary experiments to confirm the timing hypothesis: force the grow on a fixed tick, drain the pending readback before `regrow`, or defer the grow to a tick boundary. Revert each experiment before the task closes.
- Build and run scenarios as often as needed. Turn diagnostics on with `settings` overrides only. Never edit settings.ini.
- Pick between the two fix designs from the last cycle using the evidence: (a) drain and consume the in-flight readback before rebuilding, or (b) only grow at a tick boundary the throttle can't shift. Record why in the ledger.

**Off limits without asking:** the CPU solver, scenario definitions and expected hashes, render-graph/RHI/backend code, and any change to solver maths beyond contact/constraint ordering.

## Steering order (re-aimed)
1. Remove the `[regrow-diag]` logging. Pin down the first non-deterministic quantity: per-tick contact count and an order-independent vs order-dependent contact-buffer hash, to tell append-order from slot overflow.
2. Fix it by making it order-independent.
3. Judge against the criteria.

## Leading hypothesis (from the previous cycle)
The previous cycle tested and refuted the idea that the rebuilt buffers hold uninitialised memory: with a broad zero-fill, the hashes still diverged (0x49f8f35a3dd079de vs 0x021254f68f9a142c). The likely cause is that `regrow` calls `wait_idle`, destroys the per-frame snapshot buffers and clears the rollback ring, so whether the in-flight readback is consumed or dropped depends on timing.

## Steering order
1. Confirm the hypothesis: log the readback and generation state at the grow, and record a per-tick hash for a body id that is actually present. Then show that the divergence starts at the grow tick.
2. Fix it with design (a) or (b).
3. Judge against the criteria (5 runs, then the other scenarios, then Release).
