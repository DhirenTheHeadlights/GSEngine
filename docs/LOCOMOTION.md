# Locomotion

Physically simulated humanoid walking. No animation playback, no kinematic shortcuts: every motion is the result of joint servo torques, ground contact, and a balance controller deciding where to step. This document describes what works today, how it is verified, and what comes next.

## What works today

The humanoid stands up, walks, sprints, turns toward the camera, stops, and stands still — all under physics, verified by a deterministic headless test. Current measured behavior (per smoke trial, `sprint_forward_speed` 0.60): a **0.43 m/s run-window ground speed** during the 4–8 s sprint (the `avg_speed` 0.25 figure is net-displacement / 13 s and badly turn-diluted — read `sprint_speed`), mean step length ~0.35 m, lateral capture bounded, pelvis never collapsing below ~0.80 m, and a full 20 s stand-to-finish, 5/5 (trials 2–5 deterministic). Late stance rolls the heel up onto the sprung toe segment (heel-lift roll-over), and the stance ankle — not only the pelvis assist — now contributes net forward propulsion.

When sprinting, a **run push-off** fires: the stance leg extends hard at late stance and launches the body into genuine flight (both feet airborne, pelvis to ~1.03 m). The run now **sustains 0.43 m/s** run-window across the full 20 s trial, 5/5 (trials 2–5 deterministic; the smoke reports `sprint_speed` = displacement over the 4–8 s sprint window, a clean measure the turn-diluted `avg_speed` hides). **Both speed walls are broken** (next-steps §1, §0): the *lateral* wall was a step-*timing* one — walk-tuned posture gates jammed the swing release during the run's pelvis dips — fixed by sprint-blended posture gates + sprint-gated leg alternation (lateral capture now bounded). The *forward* overrun wall was likewise a *cadence* one, not a swing-power one — fixed by a faster sprint cadence (the foot keeps up with the CoM), a stronger swing-foot anchor (the foot grounds in time instead of timing out airborne and jamming), and a settled-gated backward capture deadzone (catches the slow backward topple as the body decelerates from the run). The run-window speed now saturates at ~0.43 m/s (reach-limited); the next limiter is genuine forward propulsion at toe-off, not control.

### The body

`spawn_humanoid` (Game/Game/Source/Shared/HumanoidSkeleton.cppm) builds a 17-bone rig: box pelvis/torso/head/feet, capsule thighs and shins, sphere head, box arms, and sprung toe segments on metatarsal hinges. Ball joints at the hips and shoulders, hinges at knees, ankles, toes, and elbows. There are no muscles — the rig is driven entirely by joint servos.

Two structural decisions matter most:

- **Jointed bones do not collide with each other.** The physics contact pipeline skips entity pairs connected by a joint (`add_scene_contacts_to_solver`). Before this, box corners ground against each other at every articulation and — worse — carried load: the trunk and knees were propped by corner contact, and every gain in the controller was unknowingly calibrated against that scaffolding. All current stiffness values assume the freed rig.
- **Arms spawn pre-hung and swing counter-phase.** Ball-joint shoulders are held by drives (hang pose is the spawn rest), and the leg controller swings each arm opposite its leg. Free-flopping arms measurably pumped the trunk.

### Actuation: joint drives

`gse::physics::joint_drive_component` is a per-axis angular servo solved implicitly inside the VBD joint solve — ball joints drive about each body-A local axis, hinges about their axis. Stiffness, target, torque cap, and damping per joint. Damping is implicit (predicted-angle delta, contributing to the Hessian); an explicit velocity term is unstable for light bodies and must not be reintroduced. Servo torque is an internal equal-and-opposite pair — the physical equivalent of net muscle torque, with no external force injection.

Load-bearing stiffness on the freed rig: hips 600, knees 650, ankles 400 N·m/rad, with stance torque caps 380/420/160 N·m and deliberately weaker swing-leg caps so swing reaction cannot pitch the trunk.

### The controller stack

Five systems run in sequence each tick (Game/Game/Source/Locomotion/):

1. **StateEstimator** — pelvis pose/velocity, per-foot grounded flags and support polygon (foot AABB unioned with the toe AABB, so heel-lift keeps support), mass-weighted center of mass, capture point (CoM + velocity × pendulum time), measured joint angles, trunk pitch and pitch rate.
2. **GaitScheduler** — the phase machine: idle → weight_shift → swing → plant, with capture-triggered recovery steps, turn-in-place triggers on heading error, and debounced fall detection (a single-substep contact spike is not a fall; pelvis-height collapse is). It tolerates both-feet-airborne flight (the run push-off launches the stance foot off the ground mid-swing); the support polygon falls back to the union of the airborne feet and fall detection keys on pelvis height, which stays high through a normal flight. **The swing-release posture gates are sprint-blended** (next-steps §1): the walk gate (pelvis ≥ 0.84 m, descent ≥ −0.35 m/s) holds the walk and stand, but `sprint_blend` lerps it down to ≥ 0.72 m / −1.6 m/s so the run keeps stepping through its flight/landing pelvis dips instead of jamming. The recover→weight_shift leg fallback alternates while sprinting (so the run never flails one leg) and keeps the committed stick-leg behavior at walk/stand. **The sprint cadence is faster than the walk's** (next-steps §0): sprint weight-shift/plant durations are 0.05 s (vs 0.10/0.08), cutting the dead-time between steps so the swing foot replants under the CoM before it overruns at speed.
3. **FootstepPlanner** — event-based stepping: the capture point is sampled once at swing entry and the target is committed, with at most one mid-swing refinement if capture deviates beyond a threshold. Continuous replanning chased the swinging legs' own mass oscillation into instability; commitment is what makes true-CoM capture usable. Step placement = nominal stride (sprint-blended) + capture correction + trunk-lean bias, rotated toward the desired heading (turn-by-stepping, clamped per step).
4. **BalanceController** — a horizontal velocity-drive motor on the pelvis: forward push (sprint-blended, capture-faded, launch-ramped so a standing body is never shoved at full force) plus capture braking. This is the largest remaining non-physical assist (400 N cap). Because it is a velocity drive it also *governs*: any leg-produced speed above its target gets braked with the same 400 N. The forward target therefore rides the measured forward speed inside a small headroom band above the assist target (`ride_headroom`, walking only — the band scales out with sprint blend) so leg propulsion passes through instead of being eaten. The band is narrow by measurement, not caution: headroom ≥ 0.06 m/s destabilizes the sprint window laterally, 0.04 m/s improved every stability margin while raising speed ~50%, because the motor-vs-legs fight was itself a transient source.
5. **LegController** — IK targets for stance (pose-hold) and swing (world-frame trajectory toward the committed target), written as joint drives. Trunk righting lives here: ankle center-of-pressure shift proportional to pitch and pitch rate, plus an adaptive posture trim (slow integral, gated by walk intensity — it is gait state, not posture state) split between ankle and a stance-hip bias. Late-stance ankle push and weight-shift toe-off provide ground propulsion; both fade as forward capture grows (speed regulation). On top of the push, **heel-lift roll-over**: once the pelvis passes the planted foot, an additional plantarflexion ramp (`rollover_angle`) rolls the heel up onto the sprung toe segment, and the foot anchor's vertical hold is released over the same window so it cannot pin the heel down. Engagement is gated by smoothed forward intent (stopping and standing keep the flat-foot controller exactly) and by a lateral-capture fade (no sagittal push while frontal balance is hot), and the ankle CoP righting fades out as the roll engages — righting through a lifted heel cannot move the CoP and only pumps the vault. The anchor release *latches per stance*: re-engaging a 700 N vertical hold on a lifted heel mid-roll is a slam. Stance-hip yaw drives steer the pelvis toward the heading over the planted foot. **Run push-off** (`run_pushoff_*`, sprint-gated): once the pelvis passes the stance foot while sprinting, the stance leg extends hard (knee toward straight, ankle plantarflexion, hip extension) and the stance foot anchor releases its vertical hold, launching the body into flight. Two deadzoned emergency reflexes are inert in nominal gait and fire only in near-fall regimes: stance-hip roll proportional to lateral capture beyond 0.18 m (rolls the trunk back over the support — the only frontal-plane joint actuator, since the ankle hinge has no roll DOF), and stance-knee extension blending in above 1.0 m/s downward pelvis speed (arrests the stance-collapse spiral where hot capture blocks the weight-shift release while the height-floor IK crouch tracks the sink instead of resisting it).

Heading is always active: camera facing when idle (turn-in-place above ~26° error), camera-relative input direction when moving. Sprint is a smoothed blend (~1 s), not a switch — the binary transition slammed the brakes at sprint release and toppled the body.

### Verification: the locomotion smoke

`GoonSquad.exe --locomotion-smoke` runs headless, fixed-step, with settings persistence disabled (code defaults only — see Gotchas). Five deterministic trials, each: settle 1.5 s → walk → 90° turn at 2 s (mid-launch) → sprint 4–8 s → walk → release input at 13 s with heading still held → must stand to 20 s. Each trial line reports pass/fall, plants, `avg_speed`, `sprint_speed`, `mean_step`, `mean_pitch`, capture maxima, `heel_lift_y` (max grounded-foot center height — flat foot reads 0.03, roll-over 0.15+), and a state hash. **`avg_speed` is net displacement / 13 s and is badly turn-diluted** (the 90° turn redirects the path, so a fast run can read 0.20) — for run speed read `sprint_speed`, the straight-line ground distance covered over the 4–8 s sprint window divided by its duration. Gate on trials 2–5 (deterministic, bit-identical); trial 1 is a known across-process flake and can fall or trip the reset-mid-fall crash (§5) without indicting the controller.

The state hash is load-bearing for development: trials are bit-identical run-to-run within a binary, so a hash change proves a knob did something and an unchanged hash exposes a no-op edit. Failures found in manual play get encoded as gate phases (the launch-turn, the stop-with-heading, and the sprint window all came from real falls).

### Hard-won rules

- Tune one knob family at a time against the gate; batched packages failed repeatedly from coupling.
- Rebuilds can flip marginal trajectories even with identical source — re-gate after every rebuild.
- `Engine/Resources/Misc/settings.ini` is auto-saved on exit and silently overrides code defaults in normal runs. When code-default tuning changes, delete the locomotion sections from the ini (they regenerate); otherwise manual play runs stale values.
- Instantaneous-proportional pitch feedback through the stance hip is a fold-amplifier; slow integral bias is safe. Righting belongs at the ankle (CoP), posture correction in the trim. The same applies through a *lifted* heel: once the CoP is at the toe, more plantarflexion cannot shift it — ankle righting must fade out with roll-over engagement or it pumps the vault into a pogo.
- Plantarflexion with the CoP forward of the CoM is first-order a capture **brake**, propulsion second. Shallowing the roll-over below ~−0.18 rad under-brakes the sprint into forward capture runaway, deepening past it over-energizes the vault into lateral blowout; the working depth is where the two roles balance. Before roll-over, this braking was supplied invisibly by the foot anchor's vertical hold — releasing the hold removes a hidden brake the push depth must replace.
- When an actuation regime hands off (anchored flat foot ↔ free rolling heel), every coupled term must cross the boundary together — release, righting fade, and push share one engagement signal, and the release latches per stance. A hold that re-engages mid-roll, or righting that persists past release, flips marginal sprints.
- A velocity-drive assist is also a governor: it brakes any leg-produced surplus above its target. The roll-over doubled cadence but moved `avg_speed` only 0.13→0.15 until the motor was allowed a ride-along band; with it, 0.22 *and* calmer capture everywhere — the governor fight was itself a perturbation source.
- Reflexes must be deadzoned out of the nominal cycle. A working gait is self-stable; continuous corrective feedback through it makes it worse, in every axis tried: continuous lateral-righting roll traded lateral falls for forward ones, raising the IK height floor (a permanent extension bias) broke the calibrated gait outright, and a sink-arrest onset inside the vault's normal vy band kicked the cycle into capture spikes. The same mechanisms gated to fire only in near-fall regimes (lateral capture > 0.18, sink > 1.0 m/s) pass with margin and catch real near-falls. Corollary: the smoke can prove a reflex inert (bit-identical metrics) but only a hot rollout proves it useful.
- The stance-collapse death spiral is the common terminal mode of marginal trajectories: hot lateral capture blocks the weight-shift swing release, the body sinks on stance legs whose IK targets (height-floor 0.90 = crouch) follow it down, and recovery swings are posture-gated out. The sink-arrest reflex breaks the spiral at the knee; the scheduler-level escape (hold + sinking → recover) remains untried.

## Known limits and next steps

In rough value order:

0. **FORWARD OVERRUN WALL — BROKEN (2026-06-13). The run sustains 0.43 m/s run-window at 5/5 (`sprint_forward_speed` 0.60), up from 0.36 at 0.55.** The forward overrun was the binding limiter once §1 freed the lateral wall; three leg/timing changes broke it (the balance-motor and foot-anchor forces are untouched — this is *not* a crutch increase, except the small settled deadzone below):
   - **Faster sprint cadence** (`sprint_weight_shift_duration`/`sprint_plant_duration` 0.10/0.08 → 0.05/0.05, sprint-only). Cutting the dead-time between steps drops the CoM travel per step below the foot's ~0.40 m geometric reach, so the foot keeps up instead of falling progressively behind. The doc's "cadence matched to forward velocity" — it was the lever.
   - **Stronger swing-foot anchor** (150 → 300 N). The airborne-timeout jam (below) was the swing foot *lagging* its descending trajectory, not a geometric reach limit; 300 N grounds it in the 0.30 s window so the swing never times out airborne and the gait never jams in `weight_shift`. The doc's old "anchor 350 doesn't help much" was measured before §1 + the faster cadence; in the new regime it's decisive. (Gating it by `sprint_blend` *exploded* at the transition and gating by speed broke the post-sprint walk — leave it constant.)
   - **Settled-gated backward capture deadzone** (`settled_backward_capture_deadzone` 0.10, blended to the normal 0.30 above `settled_speed_threshold` 0.40 m/s). After the fast run the body decelerates and develops a slow *backward* topple in double-support (capture_forward drifts −0.02 → −0.28) that the symmetric 0.30 deadzone ignored until the legs could no longer hold height in the lean and it sank. Catching it *only when settled* stops the topple without touching the moving gait's legitimate backward-capture (a non-speed-gated version broke the walk). This is the one motor-side change — small, and stand-only.

   The new ceiling is reach-saturation: the run-window speed tops out at **0.43 m/s** (0.60 and 0.65 both read 0.43), so above 0.60 the extra push buys no speed and only destabilises the post-sprint (0.65 falls at 10 s). 0.40 also improved (0.29 → 0.32) and stays 5/5 — the mechanism generalises, it is *not* a knife-edge tuned to one speed (the marginal flips seen mid-development were incomplete fixes; the three together are robust, 4/5–5/5 across processes with no reset crash). **One prediction below was wrong:** §0 claimed "fixing timing alone will not move the ceiling." Timing (cadence + anchor) moved it twice — first §1 (lateral), now §0 (forward) — without ever solving the "non-reactive swing" the analysis fixated on. The foot does still land slightly behind the CoM and the pelvis dips to ~0.80, but crisp cadence keeps the dip shallow and recoverable. The swing-reach/trunk-reaction analysis below stands as the map of *why* cadence (not swing power) was the way through.

   **Mechanism nailed by per-tick forward telemetry (`com_ahead` = CoM forward of support; re-add the `fwd_tel` line in `gait_scheduler::run` to re-measure).** At 0.65 the terminal chain is: a swing **times out airborne** — the foot reaches for the capture-corrected target (~0.9 m ahead: nominal sprint step + capture-step clamp 0.48, while the body runs forward) and the weak swing (130 N·m) + 150 N swing anchor cannot get it there in the 0.30 s window, so it never grounds → the trailing foot stays up → **no double-support** → the gait jams in `weight_shift` (whose release requires `double_support`) → `com_ahead` runs 0.30 → 0.86 m and the body pitches to −1.0 rad and faceplants. 0.60 is the same, just one stride later. **Resolved:** the airborne timeout was the swing foot *lagging* (anchor too weak), not the target being geometrically unreachable — the clamp already puts the target on the ground at the leg's reach. Raising the swing anchor to 300 N grounds it; cutting the dead-time keeps the per-step travel under the reach. The "non-reactive swing" the analysis fixated on was **not** needed — the foot does land slightly behind, but crisp cadence keeps the resulting dip shallow. **Dead ends ruled out on the way (don't re-try):** a sprint crouch via lowering the stance IK height-floor (the floor is load-bearing anti-sink; lowering it sinks the body *worse*); fading the push-off on high forward capture (`run_pushoff_capture_limit` — fell earlier, the flight is needed for swing repositioning); gating the swing anchor by `sprint_blend` (numerical explosion at the transition) or by speed (broke the post-sprint walk); a non-speed-gated backward deadzone (broke the moving walk). Note this does **not** unblock crutch removal (§3): the forward *braking* at the stop is still motor/anchor work, and the §3 floors stand.

   **The speed ceiling's deeper root is stance-leg height collapse during single support — measured.** Per-tick lateral telemetry (lateral capture, lateral CoM velocity, pelvis height, gait phase) on a failing fast config (`forward_speed` 0.24) showed the actual sequence: during one swing the pelvis sinks ~0.29 m (1.00 → 0.71) because the *stance* leg cannot hold height under single-support dynamic load at speed — then the low-height plant launches a large lateral velocity (vlat 0 → 0.66 m/s in 0.2 s) and the body diverges. The same sink, when slower, drops the pelvis below the swing-release posture gates (0.84/0.74) so the gait jams in `weight_shift` and topples without ever stepping. **So the "lateral wall" and the "stance-collapse spiral" are two faces of one root: the stance leg sinks during single support, and the recovery push from an off-centre, overrun configuration cross-couples into a lateral launch.** Ruled out as the lever (all measured, not theorised): stance knee stiffness/torque (900 N·m/rad, 560 N·m cap — pelvis still sinks the same), height-triggered knee extension (made it *worse* — extension from a pose where the foot is behind the CoM pushes the body forward/sideways, not up), every foot-placement reaction (§1), and banking (§1). The stance foot ends up *behind* the overrun CoM, so no leg stiffness supports height from that geometry. The real rewrite target is therefore **keeping the stance foot under the CoM at speed** — forward foot placement / cadence matched to forward velocity so the body never overruns its support — and/or an explicit single-support height-hold that works at an off-centre geometry. The lateral runaway is a *symptom*; fixing lateral stepping or timing alone will not move the ceiling while the stance leg keeps sinking. (Telemetry hooks were reverted; re-add a per-tick `lat_telemetry` line in the gait scheduler to re-measure.)

   **The chain bottoms out at the swing-reach / trunk-reaction trade-off — quantified.** Actual sprint velocity is ~1.2 m/s (roll-over + ride-along propulsion, *not* the motor target), so the CoM travels ~0.6 m per ~0.5 s step; with step length capped at 0.55 m and cadence at ~0.3–0.5 s, the foot lands ~0.6 m *behind* the CoM and the leg (0.85 m) can only hold `sqrt(0.85² − 0.6²) + 0.10 ≈ 0.70 m` of pelvis height — the measured sink. Both ways to close the overrun were measured and both fail *for the same reason*: longer steps (`sprint_step` 0.58, `max_forward_step` 0.68) destabilise and the foot only reaches mean 0.40 m anyway; faster swing (anchor 150→350 N) doesn't shorten single support enough. The common cap is **swing reach** — the swing leg cannot place the foot far enough *or* fast enough — and swing reach is capped by the deliberately weak swing torque (130 N·m hip vs 380 stance, weak *by necessity* so swing reaction doesn't pitch the trunk). So the true root of the speed ceiling is: *to walk faster you must swing the leg harder, but swinging harder pitches the trunk.* Breaking it needs swing actuation that doesn't react on the trunk — an active trunk counter-torque cancelling the swing reaction, a momentum/whip swing, or driving the swing through the foot-anchor (external, non-reactive) far harder than 350 N. That is the next real subproject; it sits *upstream* of banking, crossover, and lateral timing, all of which are symptoms of the body outrunning its own legs.

   **Every knob-level attempt to break it was tried and gated — all fail (2026-06-13).** Strong swing torque reaches avg 0.29–0.38 but always falls from trunk pitch: 150 N·m already fails (committed 130 is the ceiling), 280 reaches 0.32 then topples. The trunk reaction cannot be cancelled by any available actuator: a feed-forward stance-hip pitch counter from the swing angle is mis-phased (swing angle peaks mid-swing; the reaction peaks at swing *acceleration*) and acts as a forward lean; a stance-hip pitch-*rate* damper breaks even the committed gait at *both* signs (confirming the docs' fold-amplifier rule covers rate, not just position — the stance hip cannot hold trunk pitch, period); cranked counter-rotating arms (amplitude 0.7, 160 N·m) extend survival to ~8 s but are too light to absorb the leg's momentum; a deep sprinter knee-tuck (swing_knee_lift −1.8, to cut the swing leg's MoI) plus strong torque still falls. Foot-anchor-driven swing destabilises (800 N shrinks mean step to 0.22), longer steps are unreachable (foot reaches 0.40 of a commanded 0.58) and destabilise, stiffer stance knee (900/560) doesn't slow the sink, and earlier-contact planting (tolerance 0.20, contact_progress 0.42) reaches 0.34 at a higher push but breaks the committed gait. Net: **the legs-only actuation has no headroom left for speed; 0.23 avg is the hard architectural ceiling.** The only paths left are genuinely new mechanism — a dedicated non-reactive trunk-pitch actuator (a "magic" angular crutch on the pelvis, same class as the linear balance motor), or a separate run gait with a flight phase that doesn't rely on continuous single-support height-hold.

   **The "magic" angular crutch was built and gated too — and it does *not* break the ceiling, which pins the root precisely.** A game-layer crutch that removes a fraction of the pelvis pitch+roll angular velocity each tick (written straight onto `motion_component::angular_velocity` from the gait scheduler — the angular analogue of the linear balance motor) does hold the trunk upright, but: at a strength that holds it (0.4) it over-damps the natural gait and goes marginal (5/5 then 3/5), and at a gentle 0.2 the faster config (forward_speed 0.24) still falls deterministically. The reason is decisive: the 0.24 fall is the *overrun* — the foot lands behind the CoM and the pelvis sinks by leg geometry — which is a foot-*placement* failure, not a trunk-*rotation* one, so no trunk-attitude actuator can fix it. **This rules out the trunk entirely and confirms the root is purely that leg placement cannot keep pace with body velocity (swing-reach-limited).** Since swing reach is itself capped (strong swing overshoots into a choppy 0.19 m gait; anchor destabilises; steps unreachable), continuous walking is exhausted. The genuinely remaining direction is a *different gait* — a run with a flight phase, where the body is ballistic between stances and the legs reposition in the air instead of racing a continuously-supported CoM.

1. **Frontal-plane control at speed — BROKEN (2026-06-13). The lateral wall was a step-*timing* failure, not a foot-*placement* one.** This lateral fix first lifted the run to 0.36 m/s run-window at `sprint_forward_speed` 0.55 (then §0 took it to 0.43 at 0.60), 5/5, lateral capture bounded ±0.18. The fix is two sprint-gated scheduler changes — *no* new placement authority, no banking, no crossover:
   - **Sprint-blended posture gates.** The swing-release gates (`swing_pelvis_y_threshold` 0.84, `swing_vertical_velocity_limit` −0.35, and the recovery pair) are tuned for the walk's steady ~0.97 m pelvis. During the run the pelvis legitimately dips to ~0.84–0.90 every flight/landing, so these gates **jammed the swing release** — the gait sat in `weight_shift` (both feet down, sinking) while the lateral pendulum diverged unchecked, then the low-height plant kicked a lateral velocity and the body fell. New `sprint_*` gate fields (pelvis 0.72/0.62, velocity −1.6/−1.9) blend in with `sprint_blend` so the run keeps stepping *through* the dips while the walk/stand gates are untouched. This is the documented "scheduler-level escape (hold + sinking → recover)" — it was the lever.
   - **Sprint-gated leg alternation in `recover`.** The recover→weight_shift fallback returned the *current* swing leg when `|capture_right| ≤ 0.32`, so at speed the gait flailed one leg (lifting/dropping the same foot) while the CoM fell toward the *stance* side — the unrecoverable same-side mode. Alternating the fallback (gated to `sprint_blend > 0.5` so the walk/stand limit cycle is unchanged) keeps the correct catch-leg stepping.
   This directly vindicates the two directions flagged below as unexplored: **step timing synced to lateral state** (the posture gate was the fixed-clock blindness) was the dominant lever; the leg-selection fix is its partner. The placement-side levers (banking, crossover, wide lateral step) were correctly *not* the answer — kept here as the record of why. The new ceiling is the forward §0 overrun, not lateral: 0.65 forward-faceplants mid-sprint (lateral capture 0.09); 0.60 clears the sprint (run-window 0.39) but the body runs too hot (3.3 m/s peak) and the post-sprint deceleration diverges. **Banking from measured yaw rate is a tried dead end** (`forward_speed × pelvis_yaw_rate / g` → stance-hip roll lean, the dimensionally-clean `ω·(v/g)` form): both signs fail the gate. Positive (physically lean-into-turn) made lateral capture *worse* (0.53 vs 0.14); negative survived longer only because anti-yaw roll incidentally damps spin, not because it banks. The root cause is that one yaw-rate signal cannot serve both regimes — a *commanded* balanced turn wants lean-in, an *uncommanded* recovery spin wants lean-against, and measured yaw rate conflates them. A real bank must be driven by the **commanded** turn (heading/desired-yaw), gated to exclude recovery — but at this controller's slow turn-by-stepping the genuine centripetal demand is ~0.05 rad, so banking is near-inert until the achievable turn speed rises, which is the very thing the wall blocks (chicken-and-egg). Break the speed wall first by other means, or co-develop faster commanded turning and banking together. **Reactive foot-placement recovery is also a tried dead end** (crossover stepping + wide same-side lateral step + swing-drop suppression so the catch completes, all magnitude-gated above the normal excursion): it cannot break the wall. Two findings fell out. (a) The lateral failure has *two* modes — same-side reach limit (swing leg is on the fall side but its lateral step clamps at 0.22 while capture hits 0.47) and crossover need (swing leg on the stance side, must cross the midline) — but the scheduler's `capture_recovery_leg` already picks the fall-side leg, so crossover almost never fires; same-side reach is the dominant mode. (b) Extending the lateral step toward the fall, once capture crosses ~0.42, *catapults* the body (max_speed 6.34 m/s): at sprint speed the lateral CoM velocity is already past the single-step recoverable basin, and a far lateral plant generates a reaction that flings the body the other way. The actionable signal window between "normal recoverable excursion" (peak ~0.38) and "doomed runaway" (~0.45+) is razor-thin, so *reactive* schemes always act too late. The wall needs *preventive* continuous lateral regulation that keeps capture inside the band, not a bigger catch after it leaves — or step *timing* synced to the lateral pendulum phase (the gait currently steps on a fixed clock, not on lateral state, so wide placement can land out of phase and fail regardless of width).
2. **Walking speed** is 0.22 m/s (from 0.13 at this binary's pre-roll-over baseline; human ~1.2). Heel-lift roll-over onto the toe segment is in and load-bearing — `rollover_angle` −0.18 is a balance point, not a strength dial (see Hard-won rules), so deeper push does not buy more speed. Nominal stride is similarly walled: `walk_step` 0.40 collapses the sprint. Swings *are* sloppy — 62% of plants come from the swing timeout rather than foot-target contact (the weak swing torques, 130 N·m hip vs 380 stance, can't track the trajectory) — but **swing reach is not the speed cap**: raising the swing-foot anchor force 150→350 N lets a higher `ride_headroom` (0.06) pass that previously fell, yet `avg_speed` stays 0.22 with *worse* lateral margin. The cruise cap is the lateral wall (item 1), independent of swing reach; better swing reach only relocates the headroom ceiling without yielding speed. What may remain sagittally: real toe push-off (the toe hinge is passive — no drive, no rest stiffness, so "sprung" is a contact lever only, not an energy store). Second-order next to item 1 regardless.
3. **Crutch removal — measured, and it is §0-blocked (2026-06-13).** The pelvis balance motor (400 N) and the foot anchor motors (700/150 N) are still load-bearing; each architectural improvement so far has bought a tranche of reduction. Floors measured this session by cap-sweeping: **balance motor floors ~350 N** — below it the body forward-overruns the launch-turn (capture 0.99 at 200 N, a forward lunge, *not* a lateral catch as previously assumed); **grounded foot anchor floors ~600 N** — below it the body can't decelerate out of the run (post-sprint topple). Both crutches are load-bearing for the *same* thing: **dynamic forward braking/deceleration**, i.e. the §0 overrun. Friction is **global** (`friction_coefficient` 0.6 for every contact, set in `physics::system`; there is no per-body friction), so the foot anchor's hold cannot be shifted to real contact friction without a core-physics change (a per-body/per-material friction field, combined in contact generation, is the clean way — untried). So §1 (lateral) did *not* unblock force reduction the way the old note guessed — the binding constraint is the *forward* control (§0), and the legs cannot brake an overrun because the foot lands behind the CoM (and within-foot ankle CoP cannot reach ahead of a CoM that is 0.6–0.9 m past the foot). Simple cap-cutting only removes safety margin (~12–15%), not the underlying need. **§0's forward wall was broken (2026-06-13) but did *not* unblock this** — the fix was faster cadence + a *stronger* swing anchor (150 → 300 N) + a small settled motor deadzone, i.e. it leaned slightly *more* on the anchor/motor for the fast run, not less. The crutches' forward-braking role stands; genuine removal still needs a leg mechanism that replaces the motor's brake (a real forward foot-placement / heel-strike brake, or per-body foot friction for the hold), which remains unbuilt. The end state is propulsion and balance entirely through joints and contact.
4. **A true run gait — SUSTAINED (2026-06-13).** A run push-off is in (`run_pushoff_*` in the leg controller): when sprinting and the pelvis passes the stance foot, the stance leg extends hard (knee toward straight, ankle plantarflexion, modest hip extension) and the stance foot anchor releases its vertical hold, so the foot leaves the ground. This produces **genuine flight phases** — both feet airborne, the pelvis launched to ~1.03 m and rising — gated 5/5 with the walk untouched (launch gated to `sprint_blend` > 0.6). Two findings from tuning it harder: (a) the launch's **trunk pitch oscillates with growing amplitude** under a strong push-off and topples backward; an *attitude controller* fixes it — a "magic" crutch in the gait scheduler that each tick blends the pelvis pitch rate toward `−pitch/τ` (drives pitch back toward upright), which *stabilises* the forward launch where pure rate-damping over-constrained it. (b) But even stabilised, **the run is not faster than the walk** (avg ~0.21 vs 0.23): the push-off adds *vertical* flight, not *horizontal* propulsion, so the body's forward speed is still set by the same propulsion that caps the walk. The stride lengthens (0.31–0.42 m) but cadence drops, netting the same speed. This is the run analogue of the swing-reach wall: flight gives the legs time to reposition, but nothing yet adds forward velocity at takeoff. Next: a genuinely forward push-off (drive horizontal CoM velocity at toe-off, not just lift), or scheduler-level run phases (explicit flight/landing states with landing absorption) so the cycle can be timed for forward carry rather than emerging from the walk machine. The push-off is committed; the attitude crutch was prototyped in an earlier session (a `trunk_upright_blend`/`trunk_upright_time` pair in the gait scheduler that nudges `motion_component::angular_velocity` along `pelvis_right` toward `−pitch/τ`) and reverted. **Resolved (2026-06-13): the run sustains with the committed push-off and NO attitude crutch.** The blocker was never forward drive or trunk pitch at this launch strength — it was the lateral-timing jam (§1). Raising the push `sprint_forward_speed` 0.40→0.55 only became survivable once §1's sprint-blended posture gates kept the gait stepping through the flight/landing pelvis dips instead of jamming; with that, the run holds 0.36 m/s run-window at 5/5 and the trunk stays upright on its own. The attitude crutch remains a building block for a *stronger* push-off later, but is not load-bearing today.

   **The run breaks the walk speed ceiling — now sustained, not transient (2026-06-13).** Boosting *sprint* propulsion (`sprint_forward_speed` 0.40→0.55, NOT `forward_speed` — raising the walk push collapses the walk phase, which the sprint-gated launch never touches) drives the run-window ground speed to **0.36 m/s vs the 0.23 walk ceiling**, body genuinely airborne and carrying speed, held for the full trial 5/5. The earlier finding that it "fell every time at ~2 m/s" was correct *before* §1: the failure read as a forward-pitch faceplant **plus** lateral capture 0.33–0.47, but the lateral half was the sink kicked out by the gait jamming on walk-tuned posture gates (§1), and the forward half shrank once the gait kept stepping. With §1's sprint-blended gates the lateral capture stays bounded ±0.18 through the whole sprint and the trunk holds upright unaided. Crutch lesson that still stands: extending the attitude crutch to right *roll* as well as pitch (full trunk-upright) *breaks the committed walk* (0/5) — roll-righting kills the lateral pendulum weave the walk needs, so any future crutch must stay pitch-only or be run-gated. **Then §0 broke the forward wall too (2026-06-13):** faster sprint cadence + a stronger swing anchor + a settled backward deadzone took the committed run to `sprint_forward_speed` 0.60 / run-window **0.43 m/s**, 5/5 — where 0.60 previously ran too hot and 0.65 faceplanted. The run-window speed now saturates at ~0.43 (reach-limited), so the open piece is no longer control but genuine forward propulsion at toe-off (a real forward push-off, or a passive toe-spring energy store).
