# Locomotion

Physically simulated humanoid walking. No animation playback, no kinematic shortcuts: every motion is the result of joint servo torques, ground contact, and a balance controller deciding where to step. This document describes what works today, how it is verified, and what comes next.

## What works today

The humanoid stands up, walks, sprints, turns toward the camera, stops, and stands still — all under physics, verified by a deterministic headless test. Current measured behavior (per smoke trial): average ground speed 0.23 m/s with a sprint window, mean step length 0.29 m, max capture excursions inside the fall limits, and a gate rollout that includes a *survived* near-fall (pelvis dip to 0.69 m with lateral capture 0.38) caught by the emergency reflexes below. Late stance rolls the heel up onto the sprung toe segment (heel-lift roll-over), and the stance ankle — not only the pelvis assist — now contributes net forward propulsion.

### The body

`spawn_humanoid` (Game/Game/Source/Shared/HumanoidSkeleton.cppm) builds a 17-bone rig: box pelvis/torso/head/feet, capsule thighs and shins, sphere head, box arms, and sprung toe segments on metatarsal hinges. Ball joints at the hips and shoulders, hinges at knees, ankles, toes, and elbows. There are no muscles — the rig is driven entirely by joint servos.

Two structural decisions matter most:

- **Jointed bones do not collide with each other.** The physics contact pipeline skips entity pairs connected by a joint (`add_scene_contacts_to_solver`). Before this, box corners ground against each other at every articulation and — worse — carried load: the trunk and knees were propped by corner contact, and every gain in the controller was unknowingly calibrated against that scaffolding. All current stiffness values assume the freed rig.
- **Arms spawn pre-hung and swing counter-phase.** Ball-joint shoulders are held by drives (hang pose is the spawn rest), and the leg controller swings each arm opposite its leg. Free-flopping arms measurably pumped the trunk.

### Actuation: joint drives

`gse::physics::joint_drive_component` is a per-axis angular servo solved implicitly inside the VBD joint solve — ball joints drive about each body-A local axis, hinges about their axis. Stiffness, target, torque cap, and damping per joint. Damping is implicit (predicted-angle delta, contributing to the Hessian); an explicit velocity term is unstable for light bodies and must not be reintroduced. Servo torque is an internal equal-and-opposite pair — the physical equivalent of net muscle torque, with no external force injection.

Load-bearing stiffness on the freed rig: hips 600, knees 650, ankles 400 N·m/rad, with stance torque caps 380/420/160 N·m and deliberately weaker swing-leg caps so swing reaction cannot pitch the trunk.

### The controller stack

Five systems run in sequence each tick (Game/Game/Source/Locomotion/):

1. **StateEstimator** — pelvis pose/velocity, per-foot grounded flags and support polygon (foot AABB unioned with the toe AABB, so heel-lift keeps support), mass-weighted center of mass, capture point (CoM + velocity × pendulum time), measured joint angles, trunk pitch and pitch rate.
2. **GaitScheduler** — the phase machine: idle → weight_shift → swing → plant, with capture-triggered recovery steps, turn-in-place triggers on heading error, and debounced fall detection (a single-substep contact spike is not a fall; pelvis-height collapse is).
3. **FootstepPlanner** — event-based stepping: the capture point is sampled once at swing entry and the target is committed, with at most one mid-swing refinement if capture deviates beyond a threshold. Continuous replanning chased the swinging legs' own mass oscillation into instability; commitment is what makes true-CoM capture usable. Step placement = nominal stride (sprint-blended) + capture correction + trunk-lean bias, rotated toward the desired heading (turn-by-stepping, clamped per step).
4. **BalanceController** — a horizontal velocity-drive motor on the pelvis: forward push (sprint-blended, capture-faded, launch-ramped so a standing body is never shoved at full force) plus capture braking. This is the largest remaining non-physical assist (400 N cap). Because it is a velocity drive it also *governs*: any leg-produced speed above its target gets braked with the same 400 N. The forward target therefore rides the measured forward speed inside a small headroom band above the assist target (`ride_headroom`, walking only — the band scales out with sprint blend) so leg propulsion passes through instead of being eaten. The band is narrow by measurement, not caution: headroom ≥ 0.06 m/s destabilizes the sprint window laterally, 0.04 m/s improved every stability margin while raising speed ~50%, because the motor-vs-legs fight was itself a transient source.
5. **LegController** — IK targets for stance (pose-hold) and swing (world-frame trajectory toward the committed target), written as joint drives. Trunk righting lives here: ankle center-of-pressure shift proportional to pitch and pitch rate, plus an adaptive posture trim (slow integral, gated by walk intensity — it is gait state, not posture state) split between ankle and a stance-hip bias. Late-stance ankle push and weight-shift toe-off provide ground propulsion; both fade as forward capture grows (speed regulation). On top of the push, **heel-lift roll-over**: once the pelvis passes the planted foot, an additional plantarflexion ramp (`rollover_angle`) rolls the heel up onto the sprung toe segment, and the foot anchor's vertical hold is released over the same window so it cannot pin the heel down. Engagement is gated by smoothed forward intent (stopping and standing keep the flat-foot controller exactly) and by a lateral-capture fade (no sagittal push while frontal balance is hot), and the ankle CoP righting fades out as the roll engages — righting through a lifted heel cannot move the CoP and only pumps the vault. The anchor release *latches per stance*: re-engaging a 700 N vertical hold on a lifted heel mid-roll is a slam. Stance-hip yaw drives steer the pelvis toward the heading over the planted foot. Two deadzoned emergency reflexes are inert in nominal gait and fire only in near-fall regimes: stance-hip roll proportional to lateral capture beyond 0.18 m (rolls the trunk back over the support — the only frontal-plane joint actuator, since the ankle hinge has no roll DOF), and stance-knee extension blending in above 1.0 m/s downward pelvis speed (arrests the stance-collapse spiral where hot capture blocks the weight-shift release while the height-floor IK crouch tracks the sink instead of resisting it).

Heading is always active: camera facing when idle (turn-in-place above ~26° error), camera-relative input direction when moving. Sprint is a smoothed blend (~1 s), not a switch — the binary transition slammed the brakes at sprint release and toppled the body.

### Verification: the locomotion smoke

`GoonSquad.exe --locomotion-smoke` runs headless, fixed-step, with settings persistence disabled (code defaults only — see Gotchas). Five deterministic trials, each: settle 1.5 s → walk → 90° turn at 2 s (mid-launch) → sprint 4–8 s → walk → release input at 13 s with heading still held → must stand to 20 s. Each trial line reports pass/fall, plants, `avg_speed`, `mean_step`, `mean_pitch`, capture maxima, `heel_lift_y` (max grounded-foot center height — flat foot reads 0.03, roll-over 0.15+), and a state hash.

The state hash is load-bearing for development: trials are bit-identical run-to-run within a binary, so a hash change proves a knob did something and an unchanged hash exposes a no-op edit. Failures found in manual play get encoded as gate phases (the launch-turn, the stop-with-heading, and the sprint window all came from real falls).

### Hard-won rules

- Tune one knob family at a time against the gate; batched packages failed repeatedly from coupling.
- Rebuilds can flip marginal trajectories even with identical source — re-gate after every rebuild.
- `Engine/Resources/Misc/settings.ini` is auto-saved on exit and silently overrides code defaults in normal runs. When code-default tuning changes, delete the locomotion sections from the ini (they regenerate); otherwise manual play runs stale values.
- Instantaneous-proportional pitch feedback through the stance hip is a fold-amplifier; slow integral bias is safe. Righting belongs at the ankle (CoP), posture correction in the trim. The same applies through a *lifted* heel: once the CoP is at the toe, more plantarflexion cannot shift it — ankle righting must fade out with roll-over engagement or it pumps the vault into a pogo.
- Plantarflexion with the CoP forward of the CoM is first-order a capture **brake**, propulsion second. Shallowing the roll-over below ~−0.18 rad under-brakes the sprint into forward capture runaway, deepening past it over-energizes the vault into lateral blowout; the working depth is where the two roles balance. Before roll-over, this braking was supplied invisibly by the foot anchor's vertical hold — releasing the hold removes a hidden brake the push depth must replace.
- When an actuation regime hands off (anchored flat foot ↔ free rolling heel), every coupled term must cross the boundary together — release, righting fade, and push share one engagement signal, and the release latches per stance. A hold that re-engages mid-roll, or righting that persists past release, flips marginal sprints.
- A velocity-drive assist is also a governor: it brakes any leg-produced surplus above its target. The roll-over doubled cadence but moved `avg_speed` only 0.13→0.15 until the motor was allowed a ride-along band; with it, 0.22 *and* calmer capture everywhere — the governor fight was itself a perturbation source.
- Reflexes must be deadzoned out of the nominal cycle. A working gait is self-stable; continuous corrective feedback through it makes it worse, in every axis tried: continuous lateral-righting roll traded lateral falls for forward ones, raising the IK height floor (a permanent extension bias) broke the calibrated gait outright, and a sink-arrest onset inside the vault's normal vy band kicked the cycle into capture spikes. The same mechanisms gated to fire only in near-fall regimes (lateral capture > 0.18, sink > 1.0 m/s) pass with margin and catch real near-falls. Corollary: the smoke can prove a reflex inert (bit-identical metrics) but only a hot rollout proves it useful.
- The stance-collapse death spiral is the common terminal mode of marginal trajectories: hot lateral capture blocks the weight-shift swing release, the body sinks on stance legs whose IK targets (height-floor 0.90 = crouch) follow it down, and recovery swings are posture-gated out. The sink-arrest reflex breaks the spiral at the knee; the scheduler-level escape (hold + sinking → recover) remains untried.

## Known limits and next steps

In rough value order:

0. **The speed ceiling's root cause is stance-leg height collapse during single support — measured, and it reframes everything below.** Per-tick lateral telemetry (lateral capture, lateral CoM velocity, pelvis height, gait phase) on a failing fast config (`forward_speed` 0.24) showed the actual sequence: during one swing the pelvis sinks ~0.29 m (1.00 → 0.71) because the *stance* leg cannot hold height under single-support dynamic load at speed — then the low-height plant launches a large lateral velocity (vlat 0 → 0.66 m/s in 0.2 s) and the body diverges. The same sink, when slower, drops the pelvis below the swing-release posture gates (0.84/0.74) so the gait jams in `weight_shift` and topples without ever stepping. **So the "lateral wall" and the "stance-collapse spiral" are two faces of one root: the stance leg sinks during single support, and the recovery push from an off-centre, overrun configuration cross-couples into a lateral launch.** Ruled out as the lever (all measured, not theorised): stance knee stiffness/torque (900 N·m/rad, 560 N·m cap — pelvis still sinks the same), height-triggered knee extension (made it *worse* — extension from a pose where the foot is behind the CoM pushes the body forward/sideways, not up), every foot-placement reaction (§1), and banking (§1). The stance foot ends up *behind* the overrun CoM, so no leg stiffness supports height from that geometry. The real rewrite target is therefore **keeping the stance foot under the CoM at speed** — forward foot placement / cadence matched to forward velocity so the body never overruns its support — and/or an explicit single-support height-hold that works at an off-centre geometry. The lateral runaway is a *symptom*; fixing lateral stepping or timing alone will not move the ceiling while the stance leg keeps sinking. (Telemetry hooks were reverted; re-add a per-tick `lat_telemetry` line in the gait scheduler to re-measure.)

   **The chain bottoms out at the swing-reach / trunk-reaction trade-off — quantified.** Actual sprint velocity is ~1.2 m/s (roll-over + ride-along propulsion, *not* the motor target), so the CoM travels ~0.6 m per ~0.5 s step; with step length capped at 0.55 m and cadence at ~0.3–0.5 s, the foot lands ~0.6 m *behind* the CoM and the leg (0.85 m) can only hold `sqrt(0.85² − 0.6²) + 0.10 ≈ 0.70 m` of pelvis height — the measured sink. Both ways to close the overrun were measured and both fail *for the same reason*: longer steps (`sprint_step` 0.58, `max_forward_step` 0.68) destabilise and the foot only reaches mean 0.40 m anyway; faster swing (anchor 150→350 N) doesn't shorten single support enough. The common cap is **swing reach** — the swing leg cannot place the foot far enough *or* fast enough — and swing reach is capped by the deliberately weak swing torque (130 N·m hip vs 380 stance, weak *by necessity* so swing reaction doesn't pitch the trunk). So the true root of the speed ceiling is: *to walk faster you must swing the leg harder, but swinging harder pitches the trunk.* Breaking it needs swing actuation that doesn't react on the trunk — an active trunk counter-torque cancelling the swing reaction, a momentum/whip swing, or driving the swing through the foot-anchor (external, non-reactive) far harder than 350 N. That is the next real subproject; it sits *upstream* of banking, crossover, and lateral timing, all of which are symptoms of the body outrunning its own legs.

1. **Frontal-plane control at speed is now the wall for everything.** Turning sharply at speed was already known to need body banking and crossover stepping; the speed work confirmed the same mechanisms gate straight-line cruise. Freeing the balance motor's governor let the legs carry 0.40 m/s — and every configuration above ~0.26 m/s cruise died in the sprint window regardless of which sagittal knob produced the speed (stride, roll-over depth, ride headroom). Emergency reflexes (lateral-righting roll, sink arrest) catch near-falls at the current operating point but do **not** move this wall — at sprint energies a mistimed reflex is a catapult, and step-width/lateral-step-clamp parameterizations are bit-identical no-ops in the failing trajectories (the fall is committed before those authorities engage). What remains is real mechanism: anticipatory banking, crossover stepping, likely speed-scaled gait timing. Swing-hip roll (`swing_roll_gain` 0.3) is the proven entry point. **Banking from measured yaw rate is a tried dead end** (`forward_speed × pelvis_yaw_rate / g` → stance-hip roll lean, the dimensionally-clean `ω·(v/g)` form): both signs fail the gate. Positive (physically lean-into-turn) made lateral capture *worse* (0.53 vs 0.14); negative survived longer only because anti-yaw roll incidentally damps spin, not because it banks. The root cause is that one yaw-rate signal cannot serve both regimes — a *commanded* balanced turn wants lean-in, an *uncommanded* recovery spin wants lean-against, and measured yaw rate conflates them. A real bank must be driven by the **commanded** turn (heading/desired-yaw), gated to exclude recovery — but at this controller's slow turn-by-stepping the genuine centripetal demand is ~0.05 rad, so banking is near-inert until the achievable turn speed rises, which is the very thing the wall blocks (chicken-and-egg). Break the speed wall first by other means, or co-develop faster commanded turning and banking together. **Reactive foot-placement recovery is also a tried dead end** (crossover stepping + wide same-side lateral step + swing-drop suppression so the catch completes, all magnitude-gated above the normal excursion): it cannot break the wall. Two findings fell out. (a) The lateral failure has *two* modes — same-side reach limit (swing leg is on the fall side but its lateral step clamps at 0.22 while capture hits 0.47) and crossover need (swing leg on the stance side, must cross the midline) — but the scheduler's `capture_recovery_leg` already picks the fall-side leg, so crossover almost never fires; same-side reach is the dominant mode. (b) Extending the lateral step toward the fall, once capture crosses ~0.42, *catapults* the body (max_speed 6.34 m/s): at sprint speed the lateral CoM velocity is already past the single-step recoverable basin, and a far lateral plant generates a reaction that flings the body the other way. The actionable signal window between "normal recoverable excursion" (peak ~0.38) and "doomed runaway" (~0.45+) is razor-thin, so *reactive* schemes always act too late. The wall needs *preventive* continuous lateral regulation that keeps capture inside the band, not a bigger catch after it leaves — or step *timing* synced to the lateral pendulum phase (the gait currently steps on a fixed clock, not on lateral state, so wide placement can land out of phase and fail regardless of width).
2. **Walking speed** is 0.22 m/s (from 0.13 at this binary's pre-roll-over baseline; human ~1.2). Heel-lift roll-over onto the toe segment is in and load-bearing — `rollover_angle` −0.18 is a balance point, not a strength dial (see Hard-won rules), so deeper push does not buy more speed. Nominal stride is similarly walled: `walk_step` 0.40 collapses the sprint. Swings *are* sloppy — 62% of plants come from the swing timeout rather than foot-target contact (the weak swing torques, 130 N·m hip vs 380 stance, can't track the trajectory) — but **swing reach is not the speed cap**: raising the swing-foot anchor force 150→350 N lets a higher `ride_headroom` (0.06) pass that previously fell, yet `avg_speed` stays 0.22 with *worse* lateral margin. The cruise cap is the lateral wall (item 1), independent of swing reach; better swing reach only relocates the headroom ceiling without yielding speed. What may remain sagittally: real toe push-off (the toe hinge is passive — no drive, no rest stiffness, so "sprung" is a contact lever only, not an energy store). Second-order next to item 1 regardless.
3. **Crutch removal.** The pelvis balance motor (400 N) and the foot anchor motors (700/150 N) are still load-bearing; each architectural improvement so far has bought a tranche of reduction. The ride-along band is a step in kind: the motor no longer *substitutes* for leg propulsion in walking, it floors and governs it. A 320 N cap still drops the launch-turn (lateral catch), so force reduction waits on item 1's mechanisms. The end state is propulsion and balance entirely through joints and contact.
4. **A true run gait** — flight phases, landing absorption — is a different machine from fast walking and should be built as its own gait mode once turning-at-speed is solved.
5. **Engine debts:** the GPU solver path lacks the jointed-pair contact filter (CPU-only today), and scene reset while a ragdoll is mid-fall crashes the physics system (null deref; repro preserved, task filed — reproduced again this session: the smoke segfaults when a trial-1 fall is mid-air at reset).

## Field guide

- The smoke logs to stdout and `Engine/Resources/Misc/log.txt`. Manual-play falls leave a full trace; `ENTER FALLEN` lines plus the surrounding estimator/gait context have root-caused every fall so far.
- Controls: WASD camera-relative movement, mouse heading, shift sprint.
- All tuning lives in the settings-annotated `data` structs of the five locomotion systems and is editable live in the settings UI (subject to the ini gotcha above).
